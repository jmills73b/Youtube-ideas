# Operation Mincemeat, 1943: a short documentary made in code

This is a 4 min 14 s, 1080p30 YouTube-ready documentary on Operation Mincemeat. In April 1943 the British deceived the Germans with a body carrying forged letters, so that they would not expect the Allied invasion of Sicily.

Every frame, map, sound effect and note of music is generated by code. The film shares its drawing engine and narration voice with `../falklands` and `../somme`.

**Output** (in `output/`):

| `operation_mincemeat_1943_youtube_master.mp4` | Full-quality upload master (not committed; rebuild with `./build.sh final`) |

## Accuracy

`RESEARCH.md` gives the source for every claim and marks where accounts differ, for example over Glyndwr Michael's date of death and over how much the operation changed German deployments.

Documents on screen are reconstructions:

- Words quoted on screen, such as the opening of the Nye letter and Mountbatten's "sardines" line, follow published transcriptions.
- Summarised passages appear in [square brackets].
- The body itself is never depicted.

## Style: 1943

- **Newsreel:** 35 mm black-and-white newsreel grading for location scenes.
- **Noir:** harder black and white for the secret-plan interiors: the Admiralty, the typed letters, the wallet contents, the decrypts.
- **Maps:** styled on War Office maps, using real coastlines. Solid arrows show real moves; dashed arrows show the threats the Germans were meant to fear.
- **Present day:** the grave at Huelva is shown in colour, with its 1998 inscription appearing.
