# The Falklands War, 1982 — research notes

Working notes behind the narration (`script.py`) and on-screen text. Every
figure used in the film is listed here with its source and, where historians
disagree, the range of estimates and the reason for the choice made.

## 1. Setting

| Item | Figure used | Notes / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Distance from Argentina | ~300 miles | Nearest mainland (Patagonian coast) ≈ 480 km / 300 mi. |
| Distance from Britain | almost 8,000 miles | Great-circle UK–Stanley ≈ 12,700 km (≈ 7,900 mi); the Task Force sailed further. |

| Name | Falkland Islands / Islas Malvinas | "Malvinas" derives from the French *Malouines* (sailors of Saint-Malo). |

## 2. The disputed inheritance (1690–1833)

- **1690** – Capt. John Strong makes the first recorded landing; names Falkland Sound after Viscount Falkland.
- **1764** – France (Bougainville) founds Port Louis on East Falkland.
- **1765** – Commodore John Byron lands at Port Egmont (Saunders Island) and claims the islands for Britain; a settlement follows in 1766.
- **1767** – France hands Port Louis to Spain (renamed Puerto Soledad).
- **1770–71** – Spain expels the British from Port Egmont; war is narrowly avoided and Port Egmont is restored.
- **1774** – Britain withdraws for reasons of economy, leaving a plaque asserting its claim.
- **1811** – Spain withdraws its garrison during the wars of independence.
- **1820** – David Jewett claims the islands for the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata; **1829** Luis Vernet appointed governor by Buenos Aires.
- **1831** – USS *Lexington* raids Puerto Soledad in a sealing dispute.
- **January 1833** – HMS *Clio* arrives; the small Argentine garrison departs without fighting. Continuous British administration since.

Sources: Freedman, *Official History* vol. I ch. 1; Hastings & Jenkins ch. 1.

## 3. The road to war (1965–1982)

- **UNGA Resolution 2065 (XX), 16 Dec 1965** – invites Argentina and the UK "to proceed without delay with the negotiations … bearing in mind … the interests of the population of the Falkland Islands (Malvinas)". The quotation on screen is abridged with ellipses from the resolution text.
- **1966–1981** – intermittent talks; the islanders and their supporters in Parliament block any transfer of sovereignty.
- **1980** – Nicholas Ridley's "leaseback" proposal is rejected by the islanders and attacked in the Commons.
- **June 1981** – *The United Kingdom Defence Programme: The Way Forward* (Cmnd 8288, the Nott review) confirms the withdrawal of the ice patrol ship HMS *Endurance*; the Franks Report (1983, paras 114–118) notes it was read in Buenos Aires as a signal of declining commitment. The British Nationality Act 1981 also denied many islanders full citizenship.
- **Dec 1981** – Gen. Leopoldo Galtieri becomes president (22 Dec) at the head of a junta with Adm. Jorge Anaya and Brig. Basilio Lami Dozo.
- **Economy** – consumer-price inflation above 100% a year in 1981 (roughly 100–130% depending on series). The film says "topped one hundred percent", which holds for every standard series.
- **Dirty War** – thousands "disappeared" (CONADEP, *Nunca Más*, 1984, documented 8,961 cases; human-rights groups estimate up to 30,000). The film says "thousands", which is true under any estimate. The Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo marched weekly from 1977.
- **19 March 1982** – Argentine scrap-metal workers land at Leith, South Georgia, and raise the Argentine flag (not narrated, for time).

Wounded: ~775 British; Argentine figures vary (~1,100–1,650). The film says only "many more were wounded".
Total killed = 907, hence "more than nine hundred" in the opening.

## 6. Aftermath and legacy

- **17 June 1982** – Galtieri resigns ("within days").
- **30 Oct 1983** – elections; **10 Dec 1983** – Raúl Alfonsín inaugurated ("within eighteen months").
- **1983** – British Nationality (Falkland Islands) Act gives islanders full citizenship; Mount Pleasant airbase built (opened 1985/86).
- **1990** – UK–Argentina diplomatic relations restored.
- **10–11 March 2013** – referendum: 99.8% (1,513) for remaining a British Overseas Territory, 3 against, turnout ~92%.
- Argentina's 1994 constitution (Transitory Provision 1) reaffirms its claim to the Malvinas.

## 7. Contested questions (handled neutrally in the script)

- **The *Belgrano*.** Sunk outside the 200 nm exclusion zone, heading away from the Task Force at the time. Britain had warned on 23 April that Argentine ships could be attacked if they posed a threat, regardless of the zone, and the rules of engagement were changed on 2 May. Argentine officers, including *Belgrano*'s captain Héctor Bonzo, later called it a legitimate act of war; in Britain it stayed a political controversy (Tam Dalyell, the Ponting case). The film calls it "the war's greatest single loss of life, and still its most controversial act" and notes on screen that it was sunk outside the zone.
- **Why Argentina invaded.** Historians stress both long-standing nationalist grievance and the junta's domestic crisis (economy, repression, the 30 March 1982 CGT protests). The script presents both, without claiming either was the sole cause.
- **The name.** The film uses "Falkland Islands" in narration and gives "Islas Malvinas" alongside it on screen.

## 8. Visual research notes

- **Ships** are drawn as side profiles following the real layouts: Type 42 (*Sheffield*/*Coventry*: 4.5in gun, Sea Dart, twin Type 909 radomes), Type 21 (*Ardent*/*Antelope*), *Hermes* (Centaur class with the 1980 ski-jump), Brooklyn-class *General Belgrano* (five triple turrets, two funnels), P&O *Canberra* (white hull, funnels aft), Round Table-class LSL *Sir Galahad*, red-hulled HMS *Endurance*.
- **Aircraft**: A-4 Skyhawk, Super Étendard, Sea Harrier FRS.1, Avro Vulcan B.2 (delta seen from below), Handley Page Victor K.2 tanker (crescent wing), Westland Sea King.
- **Vehicles**: LVTP-7 amphibious tractors used by Argentine marines at Yorke Bay.
- **Places**: Casa Rosada and the Pirámide de Mayo, Buenos Aires; Portsmouth Round Tower; Grytviken's whalers' church; Christ Church Cathedral and the Whalebone Arch, Stanley.
- **Period styling**: 16mm news-film grade and grain; early-80s British TV news maps and captions; a 40×25 teletext page with block-mosaic graphics; typewritten documents; an 18th-century-style engraved chart for the history section; a halftone poster for the Argentine *Malvinas* slogan.
- **Maps** use real coastline data: geoBoundaries high-resolution Falklands coastline (CC BY 4.0) and Natural Earth 10m/50m land. Place coordinates were checked against the coastline at close zoom.
