# The Battle of the Somme, 1916: a short documentary made in code

A 4 min 36 s, 1080p30 YouTube-ready documentary on the Battle of the Somme (1 July – 18 November 1916). It covers why the battle was fought, the bombardment and the first day, the grinding months of attrition, the first tanks, and the debate over its legacy.

Every frame, map, sound effect and note of music is generated by code. The film shares its drawing engine and narration voice with `../falklands`.

**Output** (in `output/`):

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `battle_of_the_somme_1916.mp4` | Repository copy (1080p30, < 100 MB) |

## Accuracy

`RESEARCH.md` sets out the source for every figure in the film, the range where historians disagree (for example, German casualties), and what is schematic (front lines, the dugout cross-section, the recruiting-poster composite).

## Style: 1916

- **Silent-film prints.** The painted scenes are graded like surviving prints of the official 1916 film *The Battle of the Somme*: monochrome with a sepia tint (blue for night), flicker, gate weave, dust, hairs and scratches, and the 16 fps cadence of a hand-cranked camera.
- **Intertitles.** Title cards are set in IM Fell, with art-nouveau borders.
- **Trench maps.** Maps follow British trench maps of 1916: a squared, lettered grid, with German trenches printed in red. They use real river and village positions.
- **Equipment.** The artwork follows period kit: Brodie helmets, the Mark I "Male" tank with its steering tail, 18-pounder field guns and howitzers. The mines are shown throwing up white chalk, and the film ends at the Thiepval Memorial.
- **Present day.** The final two shots, the memorial and the poppies, switch to colour.

## Building

```bash
./build.sh          # ~10 min on 4 cores; needs the same Python packages as ../falklands
python3 render.py --stills   # one PNG per shot, for review
```
